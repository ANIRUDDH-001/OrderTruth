Final assembly requires a separately reviewed manifest, original approved final narration metadata/audio, and real new footage. Each product source has a media hash and can have a `proof` object (`path`, `sha256`) pointing to the sanitized capture sidecar. Its `sourceSha256`, `caseId` and `events` must match the media and manifest. Events must lie wholly within selected cuts: S06-S08 require genuine successful schema-valid fresh model investigation; S09 requires actual executed repair; S10 requires the same verified receipt reopened after reload. Receipt proof also checks one active order, matching keeper, unchanged provider evidence, and receipt identity. An AI event also requires an actual positive `aiResponses` metadata record, never historical evaluation-only output. A manifest assertion alone is insufficient. The capture adapter deliberately records zero AI requests, so its repair footage alone cannot satisfy final AI gates.

The validator checks source SHA256 and paths, exact timeline, source coverage and speed, case/orders, receipt IDs, proof hashes, narration provenance and text, and captions. Rendering retains the original sequential one-thread FFmpeg assembly and full-decode/frame-count/H264/AAC/1080p30/under-180-seconds gates. Failure preserves existing files and prevents successful final QC/artifact paths. No session or arbitrary capture contents are included in QC.

The local FFprobe executable directory was not readable under the current sandbox. Asset preparation read MP4 `mvhd` duration metadata directly with Python; hosted rendering rechecks it against FFprobe before encoding. This was a source/container read, not a render or permissions workaround.
